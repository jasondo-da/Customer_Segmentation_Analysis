## Project Introduction

The Consumer Behavior and Shopping Habits Analysis project explores data from 3,900 historical consumers, providing insights into their purchasing patterns, preferences, and overall shopping behavior. The goal of this analysis is to perform a comprehensive descriptive study to uncover trends, correlations, and actionable insights that can guide data-driven marketing strategies. By examining factors such as demographics, spending habits, product ratings, and discount usage, this project aims to help the marketing team identify opportunities to increase sales, improve customer segmentation, and optimize targeted campaigns for better business outcomes.

## Executive Summary

This descriptive analysis explores customer segmentation, purchasing behavior, and spending patterns across multiple demographic and behavioral factors.

Key findings show that male customers reorder nearly twice as often as female customers, which aligns with the overall 2:1 male-to-female customer ratio. Among the top five states with the highest number of reorders, only Illinois also ranks among the top five in total sales. This suggests that some regions maintain strong customer loyalty despite generating lower overall sales. Customers in these high-reorder states place an average of 18 to 21 reorders per person, which exceeds the overall average of 17.45. These trends indicate opportunities for targeted retention initiatives such as bundle promotions, loyalty programs, or coupon incentives.

<img width="1799" height="442" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/969acc2d-06c1-43c1-abd1-1d55fdb4843f" />

Product preferences vary notably by gender. Men most frequently purchase pants, sweaters, and coats, which reflects colder-climate buying patterns. The presence of jewelry and dresses may represent gift purchases or cultural apparel interests. Women's top-selling products include blouses, shirts, sandals, socks, and handbags, indicating a focus on casual and warm-weather apparel. These insights support the development of seasonal marketing strategies that promote winter collections for men and warm-weather bundles for women.

<img width="1504" height="427" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/6b74898b-10e1-4d77-b4b1-7435fd3c3144" />

Customer membership data reveals that subscribers spend slightly less per order but place around 4 percent more reorders than non-subscribers. This pattern suggests higher long-term engagement and customer lifetime value among subscribed customers. Similarly, customers who pay for shipping tend to place more orders and give slightly higher product ratings compared to those who choose free shipping, which contributes modestly to total revenue growth.

<img width="1294" height="425" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/33a4251a-2059-42f3-88d1-1784d6f46fd1" />

Discount usage is highest among customers aged 25 to 34 and 55 to 64, making these groups the most responsive to future promotional offers and campaigns.

<img width="729" height="426" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/083cdea2-95e0-454b-b3a2-883427155643" />

Payment behavior analysis indicates that both men and women primarily use credit or debit cards and payment services such as PayPal or Apple Pay. Women spend slightly more per transaction, while men show a higher total transaction count. These findings suggest that digital-focused marketing and online sales channels are key to driving growth, while in-store promotions could help increase cash-based sales.

<img width="1231" height="428" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/264ad2ed-852f-483a-a92d-f8f99c39150b" />

Overall, the results highlight the importance of region-specific, seasonal, and demographic-based marketing strategies to strengthen engagement, increase repeat purchases, and enhance long-term customer value.
